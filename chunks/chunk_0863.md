Now the conditional probability P(A|B) denotes the probability (or chance) that A occurs, given that B occurred, _i.e._ the chance that the combination is satisfied given that one gets married. 

And P(B|A) denotes the probability (or chance) that B occurs, given that A occurred, _i.e._ the chance that one gets married given the combination is satisfied. 

The two are different. When making predictions in real life, we are concerned with P(B|A). However, when explaining a known event in a book, we are concerned with P(A|B). If A is a large composite of many small conditions joined by “or” ( _e.g._ Venus trnasits in a trine from the 7<sup>th</sup> house or Jupiter aspects UL in transit or dasa of UL lord runs or dasa of 7<sup>th</sup> lord runs), we maximize P(A|B). In other words, A is satisfied in most charts when B (marriage) occurs. So we think that A is an excellent principle with a good correlation with event B. However, making A very large makes P(B|A) very low and we can’t predict B just because A occurred.
