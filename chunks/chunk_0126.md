This chart is also known as **Ekadasamsa** . 

**Example 18:** Let us say Mercury is at 11° in Ge and Jupiter is at 19° in Sc. We see that 11° is in the 5<sup>th</sup> part of the rasi and 19° is in the 7<sup>th</sup> part of the rasi. In the case of Ge, it is the 3<sup>rd</sup> rasi from Ar. The 3<sup>rd</sup> rasi from Ar in the reverse order is Aq. So counting starts from the Aq. The 5<sup>th</sup> from Aq is Ge. So the 5<sup>th</sup> part in Ge goes into Ge in D-11. In the case of Sc, it is the 8<sup>th</sup> rasi from Ar. Counting the 8<sup>th</sup> rasi from Ar in the reverse order, we get Vi. So counting starts from Vi. The 7<sup>th</sup> from Vi is Pi. So the 7<sup>th</sup> part of Sc goes into Pi in D-11. So Mercury is in Ge and Jupiter is in Pi in D-11 for this example. 

###### **6.2.12** **_Dwadasamsa Chart (D-12)_** 

Each rasi is divided into 12 equal parts of 2° 30' each. Bodies in the 12 parts of a rasi go into the 12 rasis starting from the rasi itself.
